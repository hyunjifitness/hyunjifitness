Fitness for
Everybody.

I coach with a long horizon in mind. Most of what strength training gives you shows up decades from now: carrying your own groceries at eighty, getting off the floor without thinking about it, keeping your balance on ice, healing well when something goes wrong. A good program should make the next forty years easier, not just the next twelve weeks.

So we pay attention to how your body feels rather than how it looks. Do the stairs feel easier? Is your back quieter at the end of a workday? Did you sleep better this week? Those are the signals worth chasing, and they're the ones that keep people training long after a number on a scale would have stopped mattering. There's no talk here about earning your food or making up for the weekend.

Bodies differ enormously — in proportions, in history, in what feels good and what doesn't — so I don't have one program that everyone gets. We start by watching how you actually move, find the version of each movement that fits your build, and adjust it as you change. My background in engineering shows up here: I'm interested in how forces travel through your joints and where you're compensating, because getting the mechanics right is what lets you keep adding weight safely for years.

And it should be something you look forward to. If you dread your sessions, you won't keep them, and consistency is the only thing that really compounds. The best program is the one you're still doing a year from now.
